# Phase 3B Mobility Network & Temporal Intelligence

## Objective

Describe the structure and scheduled-time patterns of the existing TGSRTC and HMRL mobility networks. The analysis uses only the processed, feed-scoped GTFS Parquet inputs. It does not acquire data, infer passenger demand, or fit clustering/forecasting models.

## Network Methodology

`route_stops.parquet` supplies the canonical route-stop edge list. Each edge is keyed by `(feed_id, route_id, stop_id)` and carries schedule-derived `trip_count` and `service_count`. If that table is absent, the reusable edge builder reconstructs it by joining `stop_times` to `trips` on both `feed_id` and `trip_id`.

The bipartite graph has distinct route and stop node partitions. Node keys include the partition and `feed_id`; route/stop IDs from different feeds can never connect. All rows in the `routes` and `stops` tables become graph nodes, including nodes with degree zero. NetworkX is used for graph construction; no centrality rankings are introduced.

Route overlap is generated by grouping route sets by feed and stop, then counting shared stops for unique route pairs. The analysis domain is edge-bearing routes: a route must have at least one route-stop edge in that feed. Isolated route records are excluded. Only pairs with at least one shared stop are emitted, avoiding a full Cartesian pair table. Omitted pairs within the edge-bearing domain have disjoint, non-empty stop sets and similarity zero. Pairs involving an isolated route are outside the domain and are not emitted; when both stop sets are empty, Jaccard is undefined because the union is empty, not zero. For every emitted pair:

`union_stop_count = stops_per_route(A) + stops_per_route(B) - shared_stop_count`

`jaccard_similarity = shared_stop_count / union_stop_count`

Pairs are ordered once, with `route_id_a < route_id_b`, and are never compared across feeds.

## Route-Stop Structure

The bipartite graph includes all canonical route and stop nodes. Degree is the number of linked nodes in the other partition: route degree is `stops_per_route`, and stop degree is `routes_per_stop`. `trips_per_stop` sums the existing scheduled trip counts across that stop's route-stop edges. Degree zero is retained for published routes/stops without a processed edge.

The mean routes-per-stop includes all published stops, including HMRL's 588 zero-degree stops. Its median is 6 for TGSRTC and 0 for HMRL; maximum stop degree is 812 and 2, respectively.

## Temporal Service Patterns

Weekday profiles link `trips.service_id` to recurring `calendar` flags. They count scheduled trip records enabled on a calendar weekday, not operated trips. TGSRTC has 51,985 scheduled trip records for each weekday flag. HMRL has 1,081 for each Monday-Friday, 1,002 Saturday, and 812 Sunday.

Mean scheduled trip records per calendar weekday are 51,985 for TGSRTC on weekdays and weekends. HMRL averages 1,081 Monday-Friday and 907 across Saturday/Sunday; its weekend-to-weekday mean ratio is 0.839. This is a difference between published calendar schedules only.

The hourly profile counts scheduled stop departures (using arrival where departure is blank). It keeps `service_day_hour`, `clock_hour`, and `service_day_offset` separate. TGSRTC's largest observed service-day-hour bins are hour 8 (88,460 scheduled stop departures), 16 (87,492), and 9 (87,032). HMRL's are hour 19 (4,142), 18 (4,125), and 20 (4,034). TGSRTC has 459 departure records at or after 24:00; HMRL has 120. Maximum service-day hours are 45 and 24, respectively. For example, 25:30:00 remains hour 25, clock hour 1, offset 1.

Per-route service span is `last_arrival_seconds - first_departure_seconds` from `route_service_summary`; both values are seconds from service-day midnight and extended hours are retained. TGSRTC's 4,131 routes with both bounds have a mean span of 7.34 hours, median 6.75, minimum 0.08, and maximum 41.75 hours. HMRL's three route spans have a mean of 19.48 hours, median 19.86, and range 18.60-19.99 hours. A span is an envelope across schedule records for that route, not proof of continuous operation.

Scheduled segment time is derived within each trip as the next stop's arrival minus the current stop's departure. The route feature records the mean and median of derivable segments in minutes. All 1,294,163 derived segments in these feeds are nonnegative. HMRL route mean segment times range from 1.57 to 1.93 minutes. TGSRTC's median across route-level means is 2.74 minutes. Canonical route `TGSRTC:kCuQdT4l`, short name `283X`, has a 192.5-minute mean based on six source-matching intervals. Two intervals are approximately 738.08 and 406.92 minutes; both are present in the original TGSRTC GTFS and processed stop-time data, so this is not a parsing or join artifact. Treat them as anomalous schedule values, not typical segment travel times; their cause is unknown. Another route, `TGSRTC:8qd0iyf8`, shares short name `283X` but has a very different mean (1.67 minutes), so the short name alone is ambiguous.

## Service Concentration

Shares and HHI use scheduled trip records by route, not passenger counts. For counts $x_i$ with total $X$, entity share is $s_i = x_i / X$ and HHI is $\sum_i s_i^2$ (range 0-1). It measures concentration of the specified schedule/network count across entities only.

| Feed | Top 10 routes' scheduled-trip share | Top 10% of routes' share | Scheduled-trip HHI | Route-stop-edge HHI | Stop-route-edge HHI | Top 10% of stops' route-edge share |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| TGSRTC | 8.50% | 66.56% | 0.002062 | 0.000301 | 0.000811 | 53.82% |
| HMRL | 100.00% | 40.62% | 0.365055 | 0.391298 | 0.008811 | 61.98% |

For HMRL, “top 10 routes” necessarily includes all three routes; the top 10% is the single highest-trip route (rounded up). Route-stop-edge HHI distributes distinct route-stop edges across routes; stop-route-edge HHI distributes those same edges across stops. The stop share is the sum of routes-per-stop degree among the top 10% of stop nodes. Ties are deterministically ordered by ID.

## Route Feature Dataset

`build_route_network_features(tables)` returns an in-memory route-level DataFrame with 4,136 rows and these supported fields:

- `feed_id`, `route_id`, `source_route_id`, `route_short_name`, `route_long_name`
- `scheduled_trip_count`, `stops_per_route`, `service_days`, `service_span_seconds`, `scheduled_service_intensity`
- `scheduled_segment_count`, `mean_scheduled_stop_to_stop_minutes`, `median_scheduled_stop_to_stop_minutes`
- `overlapping_route_count`, `mean_overlapping_jaccard`

Fields come from the actual `routes`, `trips`, `route_stops`, `calendar`, `route_service_summary`, and `stop_times` schemas. Missing derivations remain null where the source lacks the required records. The table is not persisted as a new Parquet output; Phase 3B creates reusable functions and the notebook displays it for later Phase 3C use.

## Findings

- TGSRTC's route-stop graph has two isolated route nodes and no isolated stop nodes; HMRL has no isolated routes but has 588 isolated stop nodes in the processed layer.
- TGSRTC route overlap is much denser in absolute positive-pair count (1,253,453 pairs) than HMRL (one pair), but the feeds are different modes and network scales; no operator ranking is implied.
- A small number of structurally central TGSRTC stops connect hundreds of routes. HMRL's stop degree remains at most two, while its no-edge stops strongly affect network-wide degree summaries.
- Calendar schedules differ across weekday/weekend profiles and show extended service-day hours. These patterns are schedule properties, not observed passenger peaks.
- TGSRTC's scheduled-trip HHI is 0.002062 and HMRL's is 0.365055. HMRL's value reflects only three route records; neither value describes passenger or demand concentration.

The reproducible tables and seven visualization types are in `notebooks/04_network_temporal_analysis.ipynb`. Its overlap heatmap selects the top 20 routes by scheduled trip records within each feed; stop plots select the top 10 within each feed. Degree and route-feature charts use distributions rather than attempting to plot thousands of individual route labels.

## Limitations

- GTFS describes scheduled service; it does not establish actual service operation, passenger usage, or demand.
- Network connectivity does not equal passenger usage. A high-connectivity stop is not a busy or high-demand stop.
- Scheduled-service concentration does not equal passenger-demand concentration. HHI here applies only to trip records or route-stop edges.
- Temporal schedule patterns do not establish passenger peak periods. Weekday flags omit exceptions because neither feed has `calendar_dates`.
- The feeds cover 2026-09-01 to 2031-09-01 (TGSRTC) and 2026-09-02 to 2030-01-01 (HMRL). These are schedule coverage bounds, not historical operation observations.
- HMRL's 588 stops without route-stop references are retained; their source reason is unknown. TGSRTC has two published routes without scheduled trip/stop links.
- Route overlap output omits disjoint pairs between edge-bearing routes; their Jaccard similarity is zero. Comparisons involving isolated routes are outside the analysis domain and not emitted; two empty stop sets have undefined Jaccard. The notebook's heatmap fills selected disjoint edge-bearing route pairs with zero and masks self-comparisons.
- Route segments are scheduled departure-to-next-arrival intervals. Missing endpoints are not derivable; the source-confirmed long intervals on `TGSRTC:kCuQdT4l` remain visible as anomalous schedule values, not typical travel times.
- TGSRTC and HMRL use different modes, route concepts, and calendar patterns. Metrics describe their own feeds and should not be used to rank operators.
- No passenger-demand data was created, inferred, or acquired. No clustering, forecasting, database, or dashboard changes were made.
